# NISP and DCSA: A Study Overview

> **Personal study material.** This is a plain-language summary written for learning. It is not
> official guidance, legal advice, or an interpretation by any government agency. Always read the
> current rule text and agency guidance at the links below. Verified against public sources on
> 2026-11-11.

## What the NISP is

The **National Industrial Security Program (NISP)** is the federal program for protecting classified
information that the U.S. Government releases to, or that is developed by, contractors, licensees,
and grantees. It was established by **Executive Order 12829**. Government-wide implementing
direction lives in **32 CFR Part 2004**, and DoD's requirements for cleared contractors are in the
**National Industrial Security Program Operating Manual (NISPOM)**.

## The current rule: 32 CFR Part 117

- The NISPOM used to be a DoD manual (DoD 5220.22-M). DoD published it as a federal rule at
  **32 CFR Part 117** in the Federal Register on December 21, 2020 (85 FR 83312).
- The rule became **effective February 24, 2021**, and contractors had six months to implement it.
- The rule folded in **SEAD 3** (reporting requirements for people with access to classified
  information). DoD later amended Part 117 to give extra time for the unofficial foreign travel
  reporting piece, which began August 24, 2022.
- Part 117 is still the governing rule as of this writing. Check the eCFR for the current text and
  amendment history; the eCFR is continuously updated but is not the official legal edition (the
  annual CFR on govinfo is).

## Roles the rule requires a contractor to name

The rule expects named people with clear authority. In study terms:

- **Senior Management Official (SMO)** - accountable for the entity's security policy and strategy
  (117.7(b)(2)). Certifies the annual self-inspection to DCSA in writing.
- **Facility Security Officer (FSO)** - runs the day-to-day security program.
- **Insider Threat Program Senior Official (ITPSO)** - leads the insider threat program
  (117.7(b)(4)). The FSO may be the ITPSO or must be part of the program.

## What DCSA does

The **Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency (DCSA)** administers the NISP on behalf of DoD
and the other agencies that use DoD's industrial security services. For cleared contractors, DCSA
oversees facility clearances, conducts security reviews, and publishes tools, job aids, and
**Industrial Security Letters (ISLs)** that explain how to meet the rule. Its training arm, the
**Center for Development of Security Excellence (CDSE)**, provides free courses and job aids.

## Self-inspections (the reason this toolkit exists)

- Under **117.7(h)(2)**, contractors must review their security program continually and conduct a
  formal self-inspection at least annually.
- A **formal written report** describing the self-inspection, its findings, and how issues were
  resolved is required, and the **SMO certifies** to DCSA in writing that the self-inspection was
  done, other key management personnel were briefed, and corrective actions were taken. DCSA's
  public guidance says the certification is recorded in NISS.
- DCSA publishes a **Self-Inspection Handbook for NISP Contractors** as a recommended job aid. Using
  it is a best practice, not a requirement. Its checklists are organized by Part 117 section.
- DCSA's public NAESOC guidance lists eight checklist areas common to all companies: 117.7, 117.8,
  117.9, 117.10, 117.11, 117.12, 117.13, and 117.16. The checklist in this repo covers those, plus
  safeguarding (117.15) as an optional section.

## Recent guidance worth knowing

- **ISL 2026-01, "Insider Threat Program: Minimum Standards" (May 20, 2026)** - restates insider
  threat program requirements under 117.7(d) and training under 117.12(g): a written program plan,
  a named ITPSO, one-time specialized training for program personnel, and initial plus annual
  insider threat awareness for cleared employees. It also says DCSA will assess compliance and that
  the program must be covered in the annual self-inspection.
